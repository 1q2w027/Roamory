# Cinematic

A short film of the trip: wide, slow, emotional. The viewer should feel the place more than follow events.

These are directions, not limits. Break any of them when the footage gives you a better idea.

- **Pacing:** unhurried, 4–8 s per shot, building to a fuller middle and a quiet ending. Let the best shot breathe.
- **Transitions:** soft fades and dissolves are home base (`fade`, `fadeslow`, `dissolve`, `fadeblack` between chapters, `hblur`, `smoothleft`/`smoothright` on camera moves). A single hard cut on a beat drop or a dip to white for a bright reveal can land hard because it is rare.
- **Motion:** slow push-ins and drifts on steady shots (a gradual zoom of 5–10 %); slow motion on movement (water, people walking, birds); a speed ramp into a reveal.
- **Look:** letterbox bars (2.39:1 inside the 16:9 frame) for part or all of the film, a gentle grade (warm highlights, lifted or teal shadows, slightly lower saturation), a soft vignette, a little film grain.
- **Text:** sparse and elegant. Thin or light weights, wide tracking, serif or refined sans (Didot, Bodoni 72, Baskerville, Avenir Next Ultra Light, Helvetica Neue Thin). The title over footage rather than on black, fading in letter-spaced; one or two quiet chapter lines ("Morning", "Day two", the city's name) that fade in and out.
- **Music:** a score that grows. 60–90 bpm, a minor or modal key (A minor, D dorian), pads or strings carrying slow chords, a piano or bell motif that comes back, a low pulse or soft kick entering in the middle, a riser and impact into the biggest shot, then strings alone to close. Original sound only for a moment where it matters (waves, a bell), mixed under or in place of the music.
