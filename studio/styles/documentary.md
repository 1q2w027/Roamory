# Documentary

A dramatic travel documentary: the cold open of a prestige nature or culture series. Grounded in what really happened, but told with tension, scale and a sense that something is at stake.

These are directions, not limits. Break any of them when the footage gives you a better idea.

- **Pacing:** a cold open on the most striking moment, then back to the start and the journey toward it. Long establishing shots (6–10 s) for scale, tighter 2–4 s cuts as a scene builds, and a held shot at the payoff. Order things to build tension, not strictly by the clock.
- **Transitions:** clean hard cuts inside a scene, a cut to black between places or days, `dissolve` or `fadeslow` for the passage of time. Let a sudden cut to a wide shot do the reveal.
- **Motion:** slow, deliberate Ken Burns pushes on wide and still-looking shots, slow motion on decisive moments of action or wildlife-like detail (a hand, an animal, water), otherwise real speed so it feels true.
- **Look:** rich, high-contrast natural colour: deep shadows, saturated skies and golden-hour warmth, a moderate vignette. Dramatic but believable; no stylised effects.
- **Text:** authoritative. Clean sans or classic serif (Helvetica Neue, Avenir Next, Gill Sans, Georgia, Charter), medium or bold weight, wide tracking for place names. A location card over a sweeping wide shot (city, country, maybe coordinates or a time), lower thirds with a thin rule, chapter titles on black. Captions state what is visibly there, with a hint of stakes.
- **Music:** a swelling documentary score. 60–90 bpm, a minor or modal key, low strings and a cello theme, a pulsing piano or synth ostinato for tension, timpani or deep percussion building to a swell at the payoff, then a release into something warm and open. Duck it hard under strong natural sound (a market roar, thunder, a street musician) and let that sound carry a moment alone.
