# Documentary

An observational record of the trip: calm, honest and well paced, like the opening of a travel documentary.

These are directions, not limits. Break any of them when the footage gives you a better idea.

- **Pacing:** steady and patient, 5–10 s per shot, in the order things happened, with a clear sense of place first (an establishing shot), then the details.
- **Transitions:** dissolves are home base (`dissolve`, `fade`, `fadegrays`), plain cuts inside a scene, a dip to black between days or places. Avoid flashy wipes unless the footage asks for one.
- **Motion:** slow Ken Burns moves on still-looking shots, footage mostly at real speed; a little slow motion for detail shots.
- **Look:** natural colour, gentle contrast, maybe a subtle warm grade; no heavy effects.
- **Text:** informative and quiet, like lower thirds and location cards. Clean sans or classic serif (Helvetica Neue, Avenir Next, Gill Sans, Georgia, Charter), regular or medium weight, modest size. A location card at the start (city, country), lower thirds with a thin rule or a translucent bar, captions that describe what is visibly there. Slide or fade them in and out.
- **Music:** understated and warm. 70–100 bpm, a major or gentle modal key, piano, plucked or marimba patterns and soft pads, no heavy drums (a light shaker or brushed rhythm at most), a simple theme that develops. Keep it low enough that natural sound can come through when a clip has good audio (a market, rain, a street musician): duck the music under it.
